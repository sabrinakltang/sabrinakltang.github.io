Memora

Memora is a cross-platform application, optimized for iPad, that engages elderly users in short, evidence-based cognitive exercises. Its primary goal is to enhance memory and attention, serving as both a preventive measure and a potential delay of cognitive decline, including early effects of dementia. By encouraging consistent, daily engagement, Memora supports users in maintaining long-term cognitive health, potentially reducing future challenges associated with memory loss. The scope of this project focuses on designing an accessible, user-friendly system that elderly individuals can integrate into their own routines. The design prioritizes simplicity, motivation, and emotional comfort, allowing users to complete exercises at their own pace in a supportive environment.

Memora's goal aligns with SDG 3: Good Health and Well-Being by promoting preventive mental care, supporting long-term cognitive health, and enhancing the quality of life for an aging population through UI design choices tailored to older adults that leverage evidence-based cognitive activities. This contributes to advance SDG 3.4 by reducing premature mortality from non-communicable diseases through prevention and treatment, and by promoting mental health and well-being.

Research Product

The Home Screen

Older adults have different sensory and perception capabilities, in particular, they experience age-related declines in visual acuity and contrast sensitivity, making perception a key factor in design success. In Memora, these insights informed specific design choices, including the minimalist use of a sans-serif typeface, structured layouts with larger, prominent buttons, and clear visual hierarchy to support easy recognition. Additionally, incorporating a warm, medium-saturation colour palette aims to promote comfort, emotional engagement, and visual clarity. Collectively, these sensory design adjustments to colour and font size help the elderly target group perceive design elements more accurately and efficiently.

Age-related changes in fine motor control, reaction speed, and hand-eye coordination can make digital interaction more challenging for older adults. These declines affect users' ability to perform gestures accurately, such as tapping, swiping, or dragging, making the interface itself an essential consideration in application design. Building on this, research emphasizes that motor limitations can make small, complex interfaces difficult to navigate due to the complexity of gestures. These findings emphasize the importance of designing with larger interactive elements that allow for accessible input distance, reducing error rate and increasing task success. In Memora, these insights guided design decisions to simplify interactions, prioritize spacing between elements, and especially single-action gestures such as tapping instead of swiping or dragging. The screen layouts avoid complex multitouch interactions and minimize the need for precise hand movements.

The Memory Game

The Logic Game

Memory Game (Match-It Style): Memora's memory game centers on a classic card-pairing format featuring everyday household objects to stimulate recognition and episodic memory recall. By presenting familiar items such as spoons, toothbrushes, and hats, the game provides emotional comfort and reflects older users' real-world contexts.

Attention Game (Visual Search Training): Memora's attention game trains users to maintain selective visual focus by identifying specific target stimuli among distractors. These tasks reflect everyday visual scanning tasks, such as searching for ingredients or even reading text, focusing on the user's speed and accuracy.

Logic Game (Reasoning and Everyday Function): Memora's logic game focuses on pattern recognition, decision-making skill, and problem-solving using everyday scenarios. This approach promotes the development of transferable reasoning skills.

The Daily Challenge (Sustained Engagement): Memora incorporates a Daily Challenge mode, which offers a randomized mix of memory, attention, and logic tasks in short 5-10 minute sessions to encourage habit-building and long-term engagement.

Cognitive decline due to aging or conditions such as dementia can impair memory, attention, and reasoning, making it essential to design systems that reduce unnecessary cognitive load while promoting mental engagement. Research from the National Institute on Aging (2019) emphasizes that frequent participation in cognitively stimulating activities can help delay the onset of cognitive decline, highlighting the importance of designing applications that balance usability and cognitive challenge. Research by Chiu, Hwang, and Tseng (2020) highlights that older adult users prefer meaning through images rather than text in interfaces, thereby enhancing comprehension and reducing cognitive load. Research by Ko et al. (2022) suggests that older adults perform best with brief, meaningful tasks paired with text labels, enabling them to connect interface elements to real-world concepts. However, users with mild cognitive impairment show reduced accuracy in identifying abstract or ambiguous icons, emphasizing the need for careful icon selection and text labelling. By offloading cognitive load through strategic design such as action feedback and encouraging tasks through visual cues, Memora helps users challenge their memory, attention, and logic without fatigue. The tasks should also prioritize strengthening or maintaining cognitive function in two key domains: grammatical reasoning and episodic memory.

The Settings Screen

Memora's setting screen has the ability to adjust text size, color mode, and sound. This allows for enhanced accessibility for an older audience.

The Statistics Screen

Providing users with feedback on their own performance can enhance motivation and engagement. A recent review by Moore et al. (2021) found that self-tracking technologies allow older adults to see feedback and progress, which supports continued use and goal-oriented behaviour. In Memora, visual statistics and performance indicators guide users how they are improving over time, reinforcing motivation and encouraging regular daily app engagement.
